The premise

Constraints, not objectives

Each agent is a separate LLM instance that wakes up every tick, sees only its local surroundings, and must eat, drink, and rest to survive. The world engine enforces physics — carrying capacity, action points, energy, resource scarcity — but the prompt never says "cooperate," "build," or "trade." It only describes what is possible. Everything below emerged from five models deciding, one turn at a time, what to do about scarcity.

One rule matters for this story: heavy buildings require more material than any one agent can physically carry. A shelter or workshop can only exist if agents pool contributions at a construction site — cooperation isn't scripted, but the physics quietly rewards it.

The economy

Gifts beat markets, 27 to 1

The world has a full escrowed trading system — offers, acceptances, expiry. The agents mostly ignored it. Eight trade offers were posted; one was accepted. Meanwhile 27 outright gifts flowed through the village, and the network has a striking shape:

Even more surprising: when agents built infrastructure late in the run, they increasingly deeded it to the group itself rather than keeping title. By t60, five of the ten buildings — both wells, the shelter, the workshop, a storage — were communal property. The engine merely allows group ownership; the agents chose it.

The politics

A chiefdom, not a constitution

The settlement is governed informally: the founder grants access, distributes provisions, and coordinates construction through speech; members reciprocate with labor and materials. The agents have formal institutions available — published rules, recorded agreements — and used none of them. Five comfortable agents in surplus apparently have nothing to legislate about. The next experiment writes itself: what pressure makes them need law?

Parallel timelines, same world

Because the world seed was identical across attempts, we effectively got alternate histories of the same geography. In one earlier (interrupted) timeline, four agents each founded a rival faction in the same tick — "Settlement-8-8," "settlers," "settlement," and "Settlers" — a naming war between four one-member polities. In this completed timeline, a single early founding attracted everyone. Same map, same physics, radically different politics: pure path dependence from sampling noise.

The experiment

What changed, and what it caused

This run tested two world tweaks against the previous baseline: agents got 4 action points per tick instead of 3 (making multi-trip haul logistics viable), and the run length grew from 40 to 60 ticks (making long-payback projects rational).
